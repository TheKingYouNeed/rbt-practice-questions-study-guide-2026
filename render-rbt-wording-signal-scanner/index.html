Five wording signals worth locating

Reading quickly can turn a small qualifier into an invisible one. A signal scan creates a pause between recognizing a topic and deciding what the question actually requires.

Negationnot, except, least, avoid
Quantityfirst, most, all, only
Timingbefore, after, during, immediately
Roletechnician, supervisor, caregiver
Evidenceobserved, recorded, stated, defined

This is a language-attention aid. A flagged word is not automatically important, and the number of flags does not predict difficulty, quality, or exam performance.

How to use the flags

SignalAsk before viewing optionsCommon reading slip
NegationAm I selecting the included case or the excluded case?Answering the positive version of the prompt.
QuantityDoes the word require one item, a comparison, or a universal claim?Treating “most” as “any,” or “first” as “eventually.”
TimingWhat must happen before, during, or after the stated event?Choosing a plausible action in the wrong sequence.
RoleWho is expected to act, decide, document, or request clarification?Selecting an action that belongs to a different role.
EvidenceWhich claim is supported by what was stated, observed, recorded, or defined?Adding an unstated cause or interpretation.
Two-pass rule: first mark the wording signal; then explain how it changes the requested task. A highlight without an explanation is only decoration.

Six original retrieval drills

1. Positive rewrite

Rewrite a fictional negative prompt as a positive prompt. State exactly how the selection rule changes.

2. Timing swap

Replace “before” with “after.” Identify which options would become impossible without adding new facts.

3. Quantity ladder

Test one self-written stem with “one,” “first,” “most,” and “all.” Explain why each word requires different evidence.

4. Role replacement

Swap technician and supervisor in a fictional stem. List the actions that change roles and the facts that do not.

5. Evidence deletion

Remove the sentence containing “recorded” or “observed.” Decide whether the remaining stem still supports the same claim.

6. One-line task

Finish: “Because the prompt says ___, I must choose an option that ___.” Use only words supported by the stem.

A compact review record

For each missed practice question, save five short fields: the signal you overlooked, the task you initially answered, the task actually requested, the stated evidence that controls the choice, and one clean-room transfer example. This makes the next review session about retrieval rather than answer recognition.

Do not copy secure or proprietary question text into the record. Describe the reading mistake in your own words and use a fresh fictional example.
