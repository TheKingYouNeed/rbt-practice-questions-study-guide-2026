A four-stage fading method

The goal is not to make every question harder at once. It is to remove one kind of support at a time while keeping the reasoning process visible and reviewable.

1

Full rationale map

Show the relevant cue, the governing idea, why one response fits, and why alternatives do not.

Highest support
2

Cue-only prompt

Keep a short prompt such as “sequence,” “source,” or “role boundary,” but hide the full rationale.

Reduced support
3

Self-explanation

Require a written or spoken explanation before revealing the prepared rationale or feedback.

Active retrieval
4

Transfer check

Use a new item with different surface details and ask which reasoning step still applies.

Lowest support

Worked example: fade the format, preserve the logic

This invented example demonstrates the editorial method only. It is deliberately generic and is not copied, reconstructed, or recalled from a certification examination.

Invented editorial prompt

A learner reviews a practice item and notices that two options could be true in different circumstances. What should the learner examine first?

  1. The option with the longest wording
  2. The exact qualifier and time order in the prompt
  3. The option seen most recently
  4. A personal assumption not stated in the prompt
Reasoning focus: distinguish evidence in the prompt from wording length, recency, or an unstated assumption.

How support changes

Full mapUnderline “first,” identify time order as the decisive cue, and compare all four alternatives.
Cue onlyShow only: “Which words determine order?” The learner supplies the rest.
ExplainHide the cue. Require a one-sentence reason before revealing the prepared feedback.
TransferUse a new invented prompt in which “best supported” makes evidence source—not order—the decisive cue.

Review the sequence, not just the score

  • Record the reasoning error. “Missed the qualifier” is more actionable than “got item 7 wrong.”
  • Restore support deliberately. If transfer breaks down, return to cue-only or full-map work for that reasoning family.
  • Vary surface details. A genuine transfer check should not merely repeat the same wording.
  • Use current official sources. Independently confirm requirements and examination information with the credentialing body.
