# RBT practice-question review workflow

This documentation describes a repeatable way to review **RBT practice
questions** without treating a raw score as the whole result. The method
separates knowledge gaps from reading and pacing errors, then schedules a
delayed retry so that improvement is easier to evaluate.

## The five-step loop

### 1. Answer before reviewing

Complete a short question set without notes. Record a confidence label beside
each answer:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| High | You can explain the choice and reject the distractors. |
| Medium | You narrowed the choices but cannot fully explain the decision. |
| Low | You guessed or relied on an unclear rule. |

A low-confidence correct answer belongs in the review queue. It may reveal the
same uncertainty as an incorrect answer even though it does not lower the
score.

### 2. Explain every option

Before reading a rationale, write one sentence explaining why you selected the
answer. After reading the rationale, compare that sentence with the explanation
for the correct option and each distractor.

Ask:

1. What detail made the correct option stronger?
2. What made each distractor weaker or inapplicable?
3. Did I apply a rule, infer from the wording, or guess?
4. Could I explain the distinction tomorrow without seeing the choices?

The purpose is to learn the decision boundary between plausible options, not to
memorize an answer position.

### 3. Classify the error

Assign one primary cause:

- **Knowledge gap:** the relevant concept or distinction was missing.
- **Reading error:** a qualifier, time order, or key condition was overlooked.
- **Application error:** the concept was known but applied to the wrong fact.
- **Distractor error:** a familiar phrase was chosen without comparing all
  options.
- **Pacing error:** rushing or fatigue changed the decision.

Use one primary label even when several factors contributed. Consistent labels
make recurring patterns visible.

### 4. Repair one gap

Choose the smallest useful intervention. Review a relevant lesson, summarize
the idea from memory, or use a brief flashcard round. Then answer a few new
questions that require the same distinction.

Do not immediately repeat the identical item just to produce a correct answer.
That often measures memory of the answer rather than retention of the
reasoning.

### 5. Retry after a delay

Schedule three checks:

- a short retry after one day;
- a second retry after several days;
- a mixed check about one week later.

Record whether the answer was correct and whether the explanation was
independent. A correct answer with a weak explanation remains in the queue.

## A practical session structure

For a 30-minute session, use:

- 12 minutes for an untimed or lightly timed question set;
- 12 minutes for rationales and error classification;
- 4 minutes for targeted review;
- 2 minutes to schedule the next retry.

Change the durations to fit your study plan, but reserve review time before
starting. Finishing more questions is not automatically more useful if there is
no time left to examine the mistakes.
